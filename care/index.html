Care guide

Nothing here is hard.
It is just unfamiliar.

Three quarters of the plants that die indoors are drowned by someone being careful. Here is what to do in the first fortnight, how to read what a plant is telling you, and the four things that matter.

The first fortnight

  1. 01

    Do not repot it

    It arrives in the pot it was grown in, in the mix it was grown in. Changing both in the same week as a house move is more than it can take. Wait a season.

  2. 02

    Expect to lose a leaf

    The oldest one may yellow and drop within a fortnight. That is the plant balancing what it can support against its new light, not a fault.

  3. 03

    Find the spot, then leave it

    Try it somewhere bright for a week and watch. Once new growth points the same way twice, the spot is right. Moving it weekly is worse than a mediocre corner.

Diagnose

What is it doing?

Pick what you can see. Most of it is one of six things, and only one of them is urgent.

Most likely

Too much water

Check this first

Push a finger in. If the soil is still damp two days after watering, that is your answer.

What to do

Let it dry to your second knuckle before the next drink, and tip the saucer out every time. Water less often, not less deeply. If only the oldest leaf has gone, that is just age and the plant is fine.

Most likely

Dry air, or a drink that never reaches the roots

Check this first

Is it above a radiator, beside a draught, or in the path of an air vent?

What to do

Move it away from the heat. Water until it runs out of the drainage holes rather than splashing the top, and group plants together so they share their own humidity.

Most likely

Roots that cannot breathe

Check this first

Lift the plant out of its pot. Healthy roots are firm and pale; drowned ones are brown, soft and smell sour.

What to do

Stop watering. Cut away anything mushy, repot into dry mix, and leave it a week before the next drink. This is the one problem worth acting on the same day.

Most likely

Not enough light

Check this first

Hold your hand a foot above the plant at midday. A soft-edged shadow is enough light; no shadow at all is not.

What to do

Move it closer to the window, or to a brighter one. Turn it a quarter every week so it grows evenly instead of leaning.

Most likely

Mealybug

Check this first

Look under the leaves and in the crooks of the stems. The fuzz moves if you nudge it.

What to do

Dab each one with a cotton bud dipped in rubbing alcohol, then move the plant away from the others and check it weekly for a month. They come back if you stop early.

Most likely

Winter, or a pot it has outgrown

Check this first

Between November and February this is normal. Otherwise, look for roots coming out of the drainage holes.

What to do

If roots are escaping, pot up one size, no more. In the growing season feed fortnightly at half the strength printed on the bottle.

The four things that matter

Light

The one you cannot fake

Every other instruction on this page assumes the plant can see the sky. A window two metres away is a different room to a plant than a window at arm’s length. Learn your rooms once and you will stop losing plants.

Rule of thumb: if you can read a book there comfortably at midday without a lamp, most of what we sell will live.

Water

A schedule is the problem, not the solution

A plant in a cold flat in February needs a fraction of what the same plant needs in July. Watering every Sunday regardless is how most houseplants drown.

Rule of thumb: finger in to the second knuckle. Dry, water it. Damp, come back in three days.

Humidity

Worth less effort than you think

Misting feels productive, but the moisture evaporates within minutes. What helps is keeping plants away from radiators and standing them close enough together to share the air they transpire.

Rule of thumb: crisp brown edges mean dry air. Nothing else does.

Feeding

Only while it is growing

Fertiliser is not medicine. A struggling plant in a dark corner does not need feeding, it needs light. A healthy plant putting out leaves does.

Rule of thumb: fortnightly at half strength, March to September, then stop.

Care by plant

Monstera deliciosa

Bright, indirect · water when dry

Splits appear with age and light, not with feeding. Aerial roots can be tucked back into the pot or left alone.
Ficus lyrata

Bright · water weekly

It hates being moved and shows it a fortnight later. Dust the leaves; they are how it earns a living.
Cuttings in water

Any light · change the water

Fresh water every fortnight, roots always covered. Move it to soil only when the roots are longer than your hand.
