Repotting, and when not to

Most repotting is done a year too early, in a pot two sizes too big, at the wrong time of year. Here is the short version.

How to know it is time

Roots coming out of the drainage holes. Water running straight through and out in seconds. A plant that dries out within three days of watering in summer.

None of those on their own mean an emergency. Two of them together, in spring, mean it is time to repot.

One size, never two

Go up 2 to 3cm in diameter, no more. A plant in a pot far bigger than its root ball sits in wet soil it cannot drink, and roots rot in exactly the way over-watering rots them.

The right time of year

March to September, when the plant is growing and can put roots into new soil. Repotting in December moves a dormant plant into cold, wet compost for three months.

How to repot

Water it the day before, so the root ball holds together. Tip it out rather than pulling the stem. Tease the outer roots loose if they are circling. Use the same peat-free mix of bark, coir and pumice, keep the plant at the depth it was, and do not feed for a month.

Expect it to sulk for a fortnight. That is the plant putting roots out rather than leaves.

When not to repot

A struggling plant should not be repotted. Address the light and the watering first, and repot in spring if it has not recovered. The exception is root rot, where the old soil is the problem and has to go.

For anything else

A photograph of the plant and one of the room is usually all a grower needs.
