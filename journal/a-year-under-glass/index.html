A year and a half under glass

What happens to a monstera between the cutting and the shelf, season by season, and why we do not hurry it.

28 January 2026

A monstera can be brought to sale size in about six months. Heat, feed, long light hours, and a growth regulator to keep it compact on the shelf. Ours take roughly eighteen. Here is where that time goes.

Months one to three: the cutting

Everything starts as a node cut from a mother plant we have kept for years. It roots in water where we can see it, which is not sentimentality: roots in water tell you within days whether a cutting has taken, and a cutting that has not can be thrown away before it costs anyone a pot of compost. What we are looking for is thick, branching root, not the single long thread that a cutting in a dark jar will produce.

Months four to eight: the first real leaves

Into a peat-free mix of bark, coir and pumice. The first leaves a young monstera puts out are entire, with no splits at all, and people are often disappointed by them. They should not be. Fenestration is a function of maturity and light, not of feeding, and a plant that splits too early has usually been pushed.

Nothing we do in these months makes the plant bigger. It makes the plant harder.

Months nine to fourteen: slowing down on purpose

This is the part a commercial nursery skips. We drop the feed, widen the gaps between waterings, and let light levels swing the way they do in a house. Growth slows visibly. Stems thicken. The distance between one leaf and the next shortens, which is the single clearest sign of a plant grown in enough light and not enough hurry.

A plant treated this way looks, at month twelve, slightly smaller than one that was forced. By month thirty in someone's living room it does not.

The last month: the acclimatising room

A greenhouse holds eighty per cent humidity and even overhead light. A flat holds neither. Every plant therefore spends its final month in a room in Hackney with ordinary windows, ordinary heating, and the draught from a door that people keep opening. Some leaves are lost in that month. Better here than in your hallway.

What arrives at your door has therefore already been through the change that kills most houseplants in their first fortnight. That is the whole point of the eighteen months, and it is the only part of it you will ever notice.
