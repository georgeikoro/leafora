How you post a living thing

A courier does not know the box has a plant in it. Everything we do to the packaging assumes that.

9 January 2026

The courier does not know there is a plant in the box. It will be stacked, turned on its side, dropped a short distance onto a conveyor, and left in a van overnight. Every decision about the packaging assumes all of that has already happened.

The pot is the problem

Not the leaves. A monstera leaf can take an astonishing amount of bending and come back. What cannot come back is a pot that has broken free, travelled the length of the box, and taken the stem with it. The pot is therefore what we address, in a moulded pulp collar cut to its diameter, wedged against the base of the box. Once the pot cannot move, the plant is mostly safe from itself.

Then the soil

Loose compost turns into an abrasive if the box is inverted. A paper disc goes over the surface, tucked under the rim, so the soil stays where it belongs and the leaves do not arrive coated in it. This is also why we water a plant two days before it ships and not on the morning: saturated soil is heavy, it sloshes, and it will rot roots in a sealed box faster than a week of neglect ever could.

Nothing in the box is plastic. Not the collar, not the tape, not the tie holding the leaves.

The leaves get gathered, not squashed

Large leaves are drawn upward and held with a paper tie, loosely, the way you would gather hair rather than bind it. They travel better vertical than splayed, and a leaf that is held cannot slap against cardboard for three hundred miles. Cut the tie when it arrives and the plant will open out over an afternoon.

The weather

Below two degrees we do not send anything. A tropical plant in an unheated van overnight comes out looking fine and turns black four days later, in your house, and the disappointment becomes yours rather than a decision we were able to prevent. For that reason, the order is held until conditions improve, and we notify you of the delay. We would rather send that message than a plant that has already been ruined by the cold.

What to do when it lands

Open it the same day if you can. Cut the tie, take the plant out, and leave it somewhere bright without repotting it or watering it for a couple of days. It has just spent seventy-two hours in the dark. Give it the week.
